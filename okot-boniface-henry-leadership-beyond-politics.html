Hon. Okot Boniface Henry:Leadership Beyond Politics

What happens when an engineering graduate chooses public leadership without leaving his professional interests behind? In this episode of The Untold Podcast, Hon. Okot Boniface Henry reflects on education, responsibility and the choices young people face as they build their futures.

The conversation moves beyond political titles. It explores balancing ambition with school, developing practical skills and recognising opportunities outside the careers people traditionally expect to follow.

From engineering to public service

Okot describes a longstanding interest in leadership, beginning during his school years and continuing through university. Rather than presenting politics as a sudden change of direction, he connects it to concerns about development in his community and a desire to contribute.

He also explains that entering public life did not mean abandoning engineering. In the interview, he says he remains involved in an engineering business with his brother. His account offers a useful distinction: changing the focus of your work does not necessarily mean discarding the knowledge you have already built.

Ambition needs discipline

One of the most relatable parts of the discussion concerns family expectations. Okot recalls his parents’ concerns about whether leadership responsibilities would interfere with his education. He describes academic performance as an important way of demonstrating that he could take both commitments seriously.

His advice is not to pursue leadership at the expense of everything else. He talks about balancing education, work, family and public responsibilities, while acknowledging how demanding that balance can become.

He also discusses continuing his studies, including postgraduate work focused on diplomacy, international relations and global studies. Learning, in his account, remains part of leadership rather than something that ends when a person takes office.

Moving from qualifications to practical skills

The interview turns to a concern shared by many students and graduates: finding work. Okot acknowledges unemployment and underemployment, then argues that young people should consider practical skills alongside formal qualifications.

He gives examples such as yoghurt production and cake-making to illustrate businesses built around everyday demand. His broader message is to look for problems people need solved, rather than treating an office job as the only worthwhile outcome of education.

That advice should not be mistaken for a promise that starting a business is easy. Our takeaway is more practical: identify a need, develop the ability to meet it and test whether people will pay for the service or product.

What public responsibility involves

Okot also explains parliamentary committee work through oversight and budget scrutiny. He describes examining whether government programmes deliver what was promised and considering how resources are allocated.

For viewers unfamiliar with these processes, this part of the conversation connects leadership to responsibilities beyond public appearances: asking questions, examining priorities and following up on delivery.

Opportunity beyond politics

Towards the end, Okot encourages young people not to limit their ambitions to political office. He discusses professional careers, entrepreneurship, content creation and technology as different routes to growth.

He also raises artificial intelligence as a force that changes employment while creating new opportunities. The message is to remain open to learning as the world of work changes, rather than assuming that familiar career paths will stay the same.

Our takeaway

The strongest thread running through this episode is that ambition needs preparation. Wanting to lead, build a business or develop a creative career is a starting point. Skills, consistent effort and responsibility give that ambition substance.

For students and early-career viewers, a useful question to take away is: What skill or responsibility can I begin developing now, without neglecting the commitments I already have?
